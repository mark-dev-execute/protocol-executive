Why I do this

I work one-to-one with people in tech when something important is coming up: an interview, a step into a more senior role, working with a team in another country, or a conversation they can’t avoid.

Before coaching, I spent more than ten years in sales operations, revenue operations, program management and consulting, at Caterpillar, Google, KPMG and Tufts University. I also founded an EdTech center and a software agency. I’ve sat in the same interviews, sales calls and executive meetings my clients are preparing for, so I coach from how tech teams, clients and leaders really work. See my full resume ↓

My sessions are practical and personal. I’m not trying to make you sound like everyone else. I want the people you work with to understand your experience and act on it.

How I coach

What to expect from me

I know international tech careers

Most of my clients work in English with teams around the world, often in their second or third language.

Your background is an asset

I won’t try to erase your accent or your style. I’ll help your experience land with the people who need to hear it.

You leave with something

A story, a script, a structure or a decision. Something you’ll use at work, not just notes.

PAR and role-play

Problem, Action, Result gives your answers and updates a clear shape. Role-play makes it second nature.

You try it again

After my feedback, you do it again, better. Changing what you actually do is the whole point.

A little work between sessions

Short, practical tasks between calls keep you moving forward.

How we’ll work

From the first call to regular sessions

  1. A free first call

    We talk about your goal, your timeline and whether I’m the right coach for you.

  2. Where you are and a plan

    In the first session I see where you are, and we build a plan around your goal.

  3. Regular sessions

    One-to-one on Google Meet, ideally the same day and time each week.

  4. A monthly review

    Every month we check your progress against the plan and change it if needed.
