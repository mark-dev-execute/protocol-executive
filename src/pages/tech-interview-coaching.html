Why it matters

Technical communication is part of the interview.

Strong engineers lose offers when their thinking is hard to follow. Interviewers are assessing how you reason, explain trade-offs and work with others — not only whether you reach the answer.

You bring the technical depth. We work on how it comes across.

  • Explaining your projects: the problem, your decisions, your contribution, the impact
  • System-design walkthroughs with clear structure and signposting
  • Framing trade-offs instead of defending a single answer
  • Asking clarifying questions before you start
  • Thinking aloud during coding without losing the thread
  • Explaining complex ideas simply to non-technical interviewers
  • Behavioral answers for technical roles, using PAR

Mock interviews

Two rounds. Two different scenarios.

Practice is only useful when it’s close to the real thing, so mock interviews are run in full and followed by detailed feedback.

Round 1

A full mock for your target role

Explaining your code, a system-design walkthrough and behavioral questions — then detailed feedback on language, clarity and structure.

Round 2

A new scenario, under more pressure

Applying the feedback from round one, handling follow-up questions and staying composed when the interviewer pushes back.

Language that helps

Phrases that make your reasoning visible.

Small, practiced structures keep you clear when you’re thinking hard.

MomentWhat you can say
Before you design anything“Before I start, can I check the expected scale and the main constraint?”
Comparing options“There are two reasonable options here. The trade-off is latency versus cost.”
Committing to a choice“I’d choose X because of Y. If Z mattered more, I’d switch to…”
Getting back on track“Let me step back and summarize the approach so far.”
Describing your role“I owned the migration plan and made the call to roll out in two phases.”

Questions

Tech interview coaching FAQ.

Do you teach algorithms or system design itself?

No. The focus is how you communicate your technical thinking: structure, clarity, trade-offs, clarifying questions and behavioral answers. You bring the technical knowledge.

Which roles is this for?

Software engineers at all levels, engineering managers, and product, data and design professionals interviewing for technical roles.

My interview is in English, but it isn’t my first language. Is that a problem?

That’s exactly who this is built for. We work on the technical vocabulary and phrasing interviewers expect, so language stops getting in the way of your reasoning.
